# Strategy Catalog: Classic Books, Professional Systematic Traders, Non-Equity Intraday Playbooks

Scope: intraday / overnight-into-day strategies codeable from 1-minute OHLCV (US Eastern time, 2008 to Oct 2026) for Lucid-approved futures (ES, NQ, RTY, YM, NKD, CL, NG, GC, SI, HG, PL, 6A/6B/6E/6J/6S/6N, ZC/ZS/ZW/ZL/ZM, HE, LE). Hard constraint: flat by 16:45 ET; entries from 18:00 ET of the prior evening are allowed.

## Honesty conventions

- "n/r" = not reported / not verified by me. Book-derived rules are written from the published logic as I know it. Where a parameter is not reliably known, it is shown as a symbol (k, N) with the commonly quoted value in brackets and flagged "verify".
- Evidence is quoted only when I could verify the source in this research pass (URLs given). Practitioner books usually show illustrative charts, not out-of-sample statistics. Treat every rule as a hypothesis for the harness.
- Every row needs our own cost model (commission + at least 1 tick slippage per side on entry stops, more on news). Several academic effects disappear after costs (see CLAS-158, CLAS-162).

## Notation

- D1 = prior trading day (RTH or the asset's main day session unless stated). O, H, L, C = today's session open/high/low/close. H1/L1/C1 = prior session's values. R1 = H1 - L1.
- ATR(n) = n-day average true range on daily bars built from the 1-min data. ADR(n) = mean(H - L) over n days.
- RTH windows (ET): equity index 09:30-16:00. CL/NG (old NYMEX pit) 09:00-14:30, settlement around 14:28-14:30. GC 08:20-13:30, SI 08:25-13:25, HG 08:10-13:00, PL 08:20-13:05 (former COMEX/NYMEX pit hours, used here as the "day session"; verify against CME before coding). Grains 09:30-14:20 ET (08:30-13:20 CT). LE/HE 09:30-14:05 ET. FX futures have no pit session; use London (03:00-11:00 ET) and NY (08:00-16:00 ET) windows. NKD: Tokyo cash session 19:00 or 20:00 ET (DST-dependent) to 01:30 or 02:30 ET.
- OR(m) = high/low of the first m minutes of the chosen session. OCO = one-cancels-other bracket.
- Default exit unless stated: MOC-equivalent = market exit at 16:44 ET (or the asset's session close if earlier and specified).

## Top 15 for non-equity futures (CL / GC / FX / grains)

1. **CLAS-114 Multi-asset intraday momentum into the session close.** It has the broadest published support: 60+ futures, including commodities and FX, over 1974-2020. The rule is simple and has few parameters.
2. **CLAS-152 WMR 4pm-fix USD reversal (6E/6B/6J/6A/6S).** Published in the Journal of Finance on 21 years of data, with a structural cause (dealer inventory). The timing is fixed, so it can't be curve-fit.
3. **CLAS-153 Tokyo 9:55 fix (6J).** Order-flow bias documented by Ito & Yamada and confirmed independently by Krohn et al.
4. **CLAS-117 CL opening-range breakout (Holmberg/Lundstrom).** One of the few ORB tests in a peer-reviewed journal, and it is on crude oil itself.
5. **CLAS-118 CL ORB with a volatility-state filter.** Large published spread in returns between volatility states. The filter works as a cheap overlay.
6. **CLAS-141 Gold London PM fix momentum.** Published in the Journal of Futures Markets with minute-level evidence. Needs a post-2015 regime check because the fix became an electronic auction.
7. **CLAS-129 CL settlement drift (14:00-14:30).** An application of CLAS-114 to CL's own settlement, which is a natural liquidity event.
8. **CLAS-125 CL EIA Wednesday straddle.** Published evidence that EIA surprises raise volatility and move returns, so the breakout has a reason to work. The edge per trade depends on slippage.
9. **CLAS-134 NG EIA Thursday straddle.** Linn & Zhu document volatility at the release that persists for about 30 minutes.
10. **CLAS-161 WASDE 12:00 straddle.** WASDE news is priced in quickly and across contracts. Grains are open during the release, so it is tradeable.
11. **CLAS-075 Fisher ACD on CL/NG.** Built by a NYMEX floor trader for energy, and the rules can be coded from OHLCV.
12. **CLAS-150 London-open Asian-range breakout (FX).** A structural change of session that every FX desk watches. Simple, and easy to falsify.
13. **CLAS-143 Gold Asian-range breakout at London.** The same structural logic in gold, which trades actively through London hours.
14. **CLAS-167 Grain close drift.** Intraday momentum applied to the grains' short day session, where settlement liquidity is concentrated.
15. **CLAS-155 Frankfurt/ECB 08:15 ET fix leg (6E).** Part of the W-pattern documented by Krohn et al. It is close in time to the 08:30 news, so a news filter is required.

## Top 10 classic systems with the most out-of-sample / independent support

1. **CLAS-114 / CLAS-113 Market intraday momentum.** Gao et al. (2018). Replicated across 60+ futures by Baltussen et al. (2021), and reported in 12 of 16 developed markets.
2. **CLAS-152 FX fix reversals.** Krohn et al. (2024). Also supported by Evans (2018), Melvin & Prins (2015) and Ito & Yamada (2017).
3. **CLAS-122 Turn-of-month.** Ariel (1987), Lakonishok & Smidt (1988) and McConnell & Xu (2008), across many countries and decades.
4. **CLAS-121 Pre-FOMC drift.** Lucca & Moench (2015). Later independent work finds it weakening after 2016.
5. **CLAS-119 Overnight drift (02:00-03:00 ET).** Published in a top journal and then independently re-checked by the NY Fed, which reports it has faded since 2021. That is useful as a decay benchmark.
6. **CLAS-117 / CLAS-118 Crude-oil ORB.** Holmberg, Lonnbark & Lundstrom (2013), plus the Lundstrom series of papers.
7. **CLAS-141 Gold PM fix leak.** Caminschi & Heaney (2014). Consistent with the regulatory findings that followed (the 2014 FCA fine).
8. **CLAS-115 Noise-area intraday momentum.** 17 years of SPY with costs included. The authors sell trading education, so the independent support is weaker. It is a modern descendant of the classic volatility breakout.
9. **CLAS-016 Williams volatility breakout.** Decades of public use and many replications. The author himself acknowledges the decay, which makes it a strong baseline even if it is not a strong edge.
10. **CLAS-041 Connors RSI(2).** Among the most independently retested short-term systems. The consensus is decay after about 2010, so it serves as a mean-reversion control.

Caveat: CLAS-001 to CLAS-012 (Crabel) and CLAS-029 to CLAS-038 (Street Smarts) are the classic practitioner core. They are widely used, but I found no peer-reviewed out-of-sample statistics for them in this pass. Treat their evidence as n/r until our harness produces it.
